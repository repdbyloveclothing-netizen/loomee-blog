Your phone buzzes from across the room, so you pick it up, unlock it and find a message that could have waited. Or you come back to a silent black screen and wonder whether anything arrived at all.

A notification light on Android gives you another option: a small glow on your locked screen that helps you tell what came in without opening your phone. Loomee makes that light personal, so a missed call can look different from an email or a message from someone you care about.

## What does a notification light on Android do?

It shows a small light on your phone's screen when a notification arrives, so you can check at a glance instead of unlocking your phone. Think of it as a simple visual cue, not a preview of your message.

A few Android phones still have a built-in notification light. Many do not. Loomee creates a screen-based light for compatible phones, using a small shape and glow on the locked screen.

That's different from lighting up the whole screen or using a camera flash. A single small light can be less startling, and the display only needs to stay on while your light is showing. You control how long it stays visible.

Loomee is designed to stay quiet when you don't want another interruption. It stays dark during **Do Not Disturb**, during your quiet hours, while you're using your phone and during calls.

The light also closes when you unlock your phone. You can set how long it stays visible, or choose a timing option that lets it remain until you swipe it away.

If your phone is on silent, Loomee stays dark unless you enable **"Light up when sound is off"**. That option is included with Lifetime access. You can read more about Android's [Do Not Disturb settings](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9069335?hl=en) if you want to review how they affect interruptions on your device.

These limits are there to make the light a quiet glance, not a reason for your phone to keep calling for attention.

## Will a screen-based light drain your battery?

The light uses your display while it is showing, so there is some battery use. How much depends on your phone and how often notifications arrive; there isn't one number that applies to every device.

Loomee rests until a notification arrives. You can adjust the brightness and set the light's timing, which helps limit how long it appears. If you want to see it in action before choosing how you'll use it, start with missed calls and try a shorter duration.

A screen-based light is not the same as keeping an entire screen lit continuously. A small indicator is meant to give you the useful part, the glance, without turning your phone into a bright panel.
