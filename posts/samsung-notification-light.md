If you miss the little blinking LED on your older phone, you're not imagining things: most newer Galaxy phones don't have one. You can use Samsung's built-in lighting and flash options, or add a screen-based notification light, so you can spot an alert without picking up your phone.

## Does your Galaxy have a physical notification light?

If you have a Galaxy S10, Note10, or a newer model, it does not have a physical notification LED. Samsung says the LED was removed as newer phones made more room for the screen and a slimmer bezel.

That means the Galaxy S10 and later S-series phones, Note10 and later Note models, and newer Z Fold and Z Flip phones don't have the old blinking indicator. Some older Galaxy phones do, including models from before the S10 and Note10 generations. Certain older A-series phones may have one too, but the feature varies by model, check your phone's specifications if you're unsure.

On a phone with a physical LED, the light is a tiny indicator on the front of the device. On newer models, Samsung offers alternatives such as Edge lighting, flash notifications and Always On Display (AOD). These can be useful, but none is quite the same as a small LED that keeps blinking until you check your notification.

## Why did Samsung remove the notification LED?

Samsung removed the physical LED as Galaxy screens expanded to use more of the front of the phone. The company describes Edge lighting as a replacement lighting option for newer Galaxy devices.

The trade-off is simple: Edge lighting can make a new alert noticeable, but it typically appears briefly when the notification arrives. It isn't a tiny light that keeps pulsing in the corner while you're away from your phone.

## What built-in notification lights can you use on a Samsung Galaxy?

Samsung provides three main alternatives: Edge lighting (called Lighting effect on One UI 8), flash notifications and Always On Display. Each works differently, so pick the one that best fits how you use your phone.

### How do you turn on Edge lighting or Lighting effect?

Edge lighting shows a lighting effect around the screen when an eligible notification arrives. On One UI 8, Samsung moved the feature to a menu called **Lighting effect**; on earlier software, you may see **Edge lighting style**.

1. Open **Settings** and tap **Notifications**.
2. Tap **Notification pop-up style**.
3. On **One UI 8**, tap **Add lighting effect** and choose an effect. Samsung's [One UI 8 guide](https://www.samsung.com/au/support/mobile-devices/how-to-use-updated-notification-settings-in-samsung-one-ui-8/) shows this updated path.
4. On earlier versions, select **Brief** if prompted, then tap **Edge lighting style**. Choose an effect, then adjust its colour and other options if available.
5. If you want to control which apps trigger the effect, review the app options on the same screen.

Some effects or menus may differ by Galaxy model, software version and notification style. Samsung also notes that Edge lighting may not work with some third-party apps. Most importantly, it's a momentary notification effect, not a continuously blinking missed-alert indicator.

### How do you turn on flash notifications?

Flash notifications blink the camera flash or screen when an alert arrives. They can be helpful if you need a more noticeable visual cue, but repeated camera-flash bursts can be disruptive.

1. Open **Settings** and tap **Accessibility**.
2. Look for **Hearing enhancements** or **Advanced settings**, depending on your software version.
3. Tap **Flash notification**.
4. Turn on **Camera flash notification**, **Screen flash notification**, or both. If available, choose which apps can trigger the flash.

Menu names can vary, so search Settings for **Flash notification** if you can't find it. Unlike a small notification LED, this option is a bright blink: useful for some situations, but not always subtle.

### How do you use Always On Display for notifications?

Always On Display keeps information visible on the screen while the phone is locked. It can show notification icons, but it lights more of the display than a small indicator would.

1. Open **Settings** and search for **Always On Display**. On some phones, go to **Settings → Lock screen and AOD → Always On Display**.
2. Turn on **Always On Display**.
3. Check **When to show** for options such as **Always**, **Tap to show**, **As scheduled** or **For new notifications**. The available choices depend on your device and software.

AOD is a good fit if you want the time and other information visible too. It is less like a notification light if your goal is simply to spot an alert while keeping the rest of the screen dark. Samsung's [AOD support page](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10001976/) explains the available display options.
